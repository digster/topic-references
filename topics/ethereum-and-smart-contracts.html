Ethereum and Smart Contracts

Last updated: 2026-08-01

Ethereum took Bitcoin's idea of a shared ledger and made it programmable. Every node runs the same virtual machine — the EVM — over the same transactions, so a program deployed to the chain (a smart contract) executes identically for everyone and its results are as tamper-resistant as the ledger itself. Users pay for that execution in gas, which is what stops anyone from deploying an infinite loop.

This is the layer almost everything else in the ecosystem is built on: DeFi protocols, NFTs, DAOs and rollups are all just contracts. It is also where the risk concentrates — contracts are immutable by default, publicly readable, and hold money, so a single logic bug is a permanent, exploitable, funded bug. Learning to write them and learning to attack them are genuinely the same skill, which is why the security material below is not an afterthought.

Good to know first: how a blockchain reaches consensus (see the blockchain page below), plus programming experience in any language — Solidity is easy to read and unusually easy to get subtly wrong.

📚 Books

  • Mastering Ethereum — Antonopoulos, Wood et al. (2nd edition) Intermediate

    The standard developer's book — accounts, transactions, the EVM, contract patterns and security — readable free online. Source at ethereumbook/ethereumbook; also sold in print.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • Ethereum Whitepaper — Vitalik Buterin (2014) Intermediate

    The original pitch for a general-purpose blockchain, written before any of it existed. Still the clearest statement of why programmability was the point.

  • Ethereum Yellow Paper — Gavin Wood Advanced

    The formal specification of the EVM, in full mathematical notation. Dense and not a tutorial, but it is the authority when the docs are ambiguous.

  • Ethereum Developer Documentation — ethereum.org Beginner

    The maintained reference for the whole stack, and the correct default answer to most "how does X work" questions. Start with accounts, gas and the EVM.

  • Solidity Documentation — Solidity team Intermediate

    The language reference. The "Security Considerations" and "Common Patterns" chapters are worth reading start to finish before you deploy anything.

  • EIP-20: Token Standard — Fabian Vogelsteller & Vitalik Buterin (2015) Intermediate

    Six functions and two events that became the interface every fungible token implements. A short, instructive example of how a standard beats a protocol change.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • Introduction to Smart Contracts — ethereum.org Beginner

    What a contract is, what it can and can't do, and the limitations people usually discover the hard way — no randomness, no scheduled execution, no outside data.

  • Solidity Patterns — Franz Volland Intermediate

    A catalogue of the standard idioms — checks-effects-interactions, pull payments, guard checks — each with rationale and sample code. Short and high value per page.

  • Smart Contract Best Practices — Consensys Diligence Intermediate

    The long-running practitioner guide to known attacks and defensive patterns, maintained by a team that audits for a living.

  • CRITICAL UPDATE Re: DAO Vulnerability — Vitalik Buterin, Ethereum Foundation (2016) Intermediate

    The live post announcing the reentrancy exploit that drained The DAO and split the chain. The founding document of smart-contract security, written mid-crisis.

  • samczsun's blog — samczsun Advanced

    First-hand write-ups of real vulnerabilities found and disclosed, with the reasoning shown. The closest thing to watching an expert think about contract security.

  • The Three Transitions — Vitalik Buterin (2023) Advanced

    Why L2 scaling, smart-contract wallets and privacy have to happen together, and what breaks if they don't. Useful for seeing where contract development is headed.

🎥 Videos & Talks

  • Smart contracts — Simply Explained — Simply Explained Beginner

    Five minutes, no jargon, no token pitch. The right thing to send someone who has heard the phrase and wants to know what it actually means.

  • Code Is Law? Smart Contracts Explained — Finematics Beginner

    Covers the mechanics and then the uncomfortable part — what "code is law" meant in practice when The DAO was drained and the chain forked anyway.

  • Ethereum in 30 minutes — Vitalik Buterin, Devcon SEA (Ethereum Foundation) Intermediate

    The whole system explained by its founder in one sitting — what it is, what it's for, and which parts he thinks are still unsolved.

  • CryptoZombies — Loom Network Beginner

    Free interactive Solidity lessons framed as building a zombie game. Silly, effective, and the standard first thing people do.

  • Solidity by Example — solidity-by-example.org Beginner

    Every language feature and common pattern as a minimal annotated contract. The fastest reference when you half-remember the syntax.

  • Speed Run Ethereum — BuidlGuidl Intermediate

    A free, genuinely comprehensive curriculum from Solidity basics through Foundry, advanced patterns and security auditing. The most complete free path available.

  • Learn Solidity — RareSkills Advanced

    For people who can already write contracts and want gas optimisation, assembly and the EVM-level detail underneath the language.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • The Foundry Book — Foundry Intermediate

    Documentation for the toolchain most teams now use — Forge for building and testing in Solidity itself, Cast for chain interaction, Anvil for a local node.

  • Hardhat — Nomic Foundation Intermediate

    The JavaScript/TypeScript development environment, with the richest plugin ecosystem. Still the right choice if your stack is already Node.

  • OpenZeppelin Contracts — OpenZeppelin Intermediate

    The audited standard-library implementations of tokens, access control and upgradeability. Reading them is also one of the better ways to learn idiomatic Solidity.

  • evm.codes — Smlxl Advanced

    An interactive opcode reference with gas costs and a playground that steps through execution. Indispensable once you're reading bytecode or writing assembly.

  • Ethernaut — OpenZeppelin Intermediate

    A wargame where each level is a deployed contract you have to break. The standard way to convert "I know the vulnerabilities" into "I can find them".

  • Damn Vulnerable DeFi — The Red Guild Advanced

    The harder sequel: flash loans, oracle manipulation, governance takeovers and vault exploits, all as Foundry challenges. Do Ethernaut first.

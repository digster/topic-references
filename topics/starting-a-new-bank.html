Starting a New Bank

Last updated: 2026-07-29

Starting a bank splits into two paths almost everywhere in the world. You can get your own licence — a de novo bank that holds regulated capital, takes deposits and lends against them, which is slow and capital-heavy — or you can build on someone else's licence, running a neobank or embedded-finance product on top of a partner or "sponsor" bank through Banking-as-a-Service APIs. Almost every modern "bank" you can name started on the second path; a growing number are now buying their way onto the first.

Underneath, the business is the same in every jurisdiction: gather deposits cheaply, lend or invest them profitably (the net interest margin), move money safely, earn fees, and stay solvent and trusted while doing it. What changes by country is the licence — the FDIC and OCC in the US, the PRA and FCA in the UK, the RBI in India — and whether a standalone digital bank is a thing the regulator will even license. The resources below lead with the business and the plumbing, then point at the specific rulebooks.

Good to know first: a little balance-sheet literacy (assets vs. liabilities, deposits vs. loans) and a rough mental model of how a card payment moves money will make everything below click faster — but neither is required.

📚 Books

  • Banking On It: How I Disrupted an Industry — Anne Boden, Penguin (2020) Beginner

    The founder of Starling on actually getting a banking licence and building the bank behind it — the closest thing to a first-hand log of the whole process. Paid.

  • Bank 4.0: Banking Everywhere, Never at a Bank — Brett King, Wiley (2018) Beginner

    The argument that banking is becoming a utility embedded in software rather than a place you visit — the worldview most neobanks are built on. Paid.

  • The Anatomy of the Swipe: Making Money Move — Ahmed Siddiqui (2020) Intermediate

    A builder's tour of what happens between tapping a card and money landing — issuers, acquirers, networks, interchange. Essential if you'll issue accounts or cards. Paid.

  • Payments Systems in the U.S. — Carol Coye Benson, Scott Loftesness & Russ Jones, Glenbrook (3rd ed.) Intermediate

    The reference that explains every US payment rail — cards, ACH, wires, real-time — in one consistent vocabulary. US-specific, but the mental model transfers. Paid.

  • The Bankers' New Clothes — Anat Admati & Martin Hellwig, Princeton University Press Advanced

    Why bank capital exists and why thin equity keeps blowing banks up, argued without jargon. Read it before deciding how much of your balance sheet to fund with debt. Paid.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • New Bank Start-up Unit — Bank of England (PRA) & FCA, United Kingdom Intermediate

    The most readable "how to start a bank" material any regulator publishes: should-I-become-a-bank guides, alternatives, business-plan expectations, and the mobilisation route that lets you authorise with restrictions first.

  • Applying for Deposit Insurance — A Handbook for Organizers of De Novo Institutions — FDIC, United States Intermediate

    The US equivalent, written for organizers: what a de novo application contains, what examiners look for, and how long each stage takes.

  • Guidelines for 'on tap' Licensing of Universal Banks in the Private Sector — Reserve Bank of India (2016) Intermediate

    India's standing framework for new private banks — eligible promoters, ₹5bn minimum capital, shareholding lock-ins. Worth reading alongside the fact that the RBI still licenses no standalone neobanks, so Indian neobanks all sit on a partner bank.

  • Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management — Federal Reserve, FDIC & OCC (2023) Advanced

    The rules a sponsor bank is judged against when it lets you build on its charter — which is to say, the real constraints behind every BaaS contract you'll sign.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • Every Company Will Be a Fintech Company — Angela Strange, Andreessen Horowitz (2020) Beginner

    The essay that named the shift: financial infrastructure became available as APIs, so launching banking products stopped requiring a bank.

  • The alchemy of deposits — Patrick McKenzie, Bits about Money (2022) Intermediate

    What a deposit actually is, why it is the most valuable liability in business, and why the whole edifice of regulation exists to keep it money-good. Start here for the economics.

  • Seeing like a Bank — Patrick McKenzie, Bits about Money Intermediate

    How banks perceive customers, risk and compliance from the inside — the mindset that explains decisions that look irrational from a product team's seat.

  • A Fintech Guide to Bank Partners and Sponsors — Lithic Intermediate

    The practical version of the second path: how to find and vet a sponsor bank, the partnership models available, and what each one costs you in control.

  • Everyone wants to be a bank — Simon Taylor, Fintech Brainfood (2026) Intermediate

    The current turn: after the sponsor-bank shocks, fintechs are buying charters for the economics and control. The clearest read on when owning the licence is worth it.

🎥 Videos & Talks

  • Banking Explained — Money and Credit — Kurzgesagt Beginner

    Six animated minutes on what banks do with your money and how lending creates it. The fastest possible foundation if the balance sheet is new to you.

  • Building the bank of the future with Monzo, N26 and Revolut — TechCrunch Disrupt Berlin (2017) Intermediate

    Three founders comparing routes at the moment they were taking them — prepaid card on someone else's licence, full charter, EU passporting. Dated in detail, still the best account of the decision.

  • What to Expect in Fintech's Next 10 Years — Angela Strange, fintech_devcon (2021) Intermediate

    The infrastructure thesis delivered to builders, with the stack laid out layer by layer — a good map of which parts you'd buy rather than build.

  • Bank on it — Financial Service Firm Valuation — Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern Advanced

    Why banks are valued differently from every other business — debt is raw material, regulation is a constraint, and book value actually means something.

🎓 Courses & Tutorials

  • Banking & Money — Khan Academy Beginner

    Free short videos building the fundamentals from zero: bank balance sheets, fractional reserve lending, and how deposits become loans.

  • Modern Treasury Learn — Modern Treasury Beginner

    Free primers on the money-movement vocabulary you'll be expected to know — payment rails, ACH, FedNow, reconciliation, ledgers — each a short standalone read.

  • Payments Boot Camp — Glenbrook Partners Intermediate

    The industry's standard two-day grounding in how payments work and who earns what along the way, in person or on demand. Paid, and priced for employers — the free alternative is the Modern Treasury primers above.

  • Financial Technology (Fintech) Innovations Specialization — University of Michigan, Coursera Beginner

    Four structured courses across payments, blockchain, credit and investment tech. Paid — the FAQ states it cannot be audited free, though financial aid is available.

  • Valuation — free online class — Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern Advanced

    Twenty-five free sessions with slides and tests, including the financial-services material. Take it if you need to defend your own numbers to an investor or a regulator.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • Column — Nationally chartered US bank with a developer API

    A bank that owns its charter and exposes it directly — the clearest example of what disappears from the stack when there's no middleman between you and the rails.

  • Unit — Embedded-finance platform

    Accounts, cards and payments by API on top of partner banks. Read the docs to see exactly which primitives BaaS hands you out of the box.

  • Stripe Treasury — Banking-as-a-service by Stripe

    Lets a platform embed FDIC-insured accounts and payouts for its own customers — the most productized version of "a bank as an API".

  • Marqeta — Modern card issuing

    The issuing layer behind many fintech debit and credit programs; its docs are a practical education in authorizations, controls and card economics.

  • FDIC BankFind Suite — FDIC

    Every US bank's real financials, free: deposits, loans, capital, income. The fastest way to sanity-check your business plan against banks that already exist.

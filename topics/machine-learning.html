Machine Learning

Last updated: 2026-07-29

Machine learning is the practice of fitting models to data so they make useful predictions about examples they have never seen, instead of following rules a programmer wrote out by hand. You pick a model family — a linear model, a decision tree, an ensemble of trees — and an algorithm searches for the parameters that best explain the training data. The central question is never "does it fit?" but does it generalise?

Fitting the model is usually the easy part. Most of the difficulty lives around it: getting clean and representative data, building features that carry signal, and evaluating honestly enough to know whether a result will survive contact with reality. And a model that ships becomes a system — one that has to be monitored, retrained and debugged as the data underneath it drifts. This page follows that whole arc, staying on classical (largely tabular) machine learning and the practice of running it in production; deep learning has its own page.

Good to know first: comfortable Python, a little linear algebra (vectors, matrices) and probability/statistics (distributions, expectation, variance). Some pandas and NumPy if you want to follow the hands-on resources.

📚 Books

  • The Hundred-Page Machine Learning Book — Andriy Burkov (2019, still maintained) Beginner

    Genuinely about a hundred pages, and it names every important idea without drowning you in any of them. Read-first-buy-later: chapters are free to download, the book is paid.

  • An Introduction to Statistical Learning — James, Witten, Hastie & Tibshirani, Springer (Python ed. 2023) Beginner

    The standard first textbook, and the single best use of your time here. Free PDF on the site, in both Python (ISLP) and R (ISLR) editions, with labs for each.

  • Hands-On Machine Learning with Scikit-Learn, Keras & TensorFlow (3rd ed.) — Aurélien Géron, O'Reilly (2022) Intermediate

    Part I is the reason it's here: the most practical tour of classical ML in scikit-learn there is, end-to-end project included. Paid, but every notebook is free at ageron/handson-ml3.

  • Designing Machine Learning Systems — Chip Huyen, O'Reilly (2022) Intermediate

    The book about everything that surrounds the model — data pipelines, training-serving skew, deployment, drift, monitoring. Paid; the author's earlier free notes are at Machine Learning Systems Design.

  • The Elements of Statistical Learning (2nd ed.) — Hastie, Tibshirani & Friedman, Springer (2009) Advanced

    ISL's rigorous older sibling and the field's reference text — same authors, full mathematics. The free PDF is linked from the book's download page.

  • Understanding Machine Learning: From Theory to Algorithms — Shalev-Shwartz & Ben-David, Cambridge University Press (2014) Advanced

    Where "why does generalisation work at all?" gets a real answer: PAC learning, VC dimension, and the bias-complexity tradeoff. Full book free as a PDF from the authors.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • A Few Useful Things to Know About Machine Learning — Pedro Domingos, Communications of the ACM (2012) Intermediate

    Ten pages of the folk wisdom that usually takes years to accumulate — why more data beats a cleverer algorithm, why intuition fails in high dimensions. Read it early, then again later.

  • Statistical Modeling: The Two Cultures — Leo Breiman, Statistical Science (2001) Advanced

    The essay that named the split between assuming a data model and simply predicting well — and largely called how the field would go. Free course copy; the journal version on Project Euclid is paywalled.

  • Random Forests — Leo Breiman, Machine Learning (2001) Advanced

    The original paper for the algorithm that is still a superb default on tabular data, and still the best explanation of why averaging decorrelated trees works.

  • XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System — Chen & Guestrin, KDD (2016) Advanced

    Gradient-boosted trees remain the thing to beat on tabular problems. This is the paper behind the implementation that made them fast enough to win everything.

  • Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems — Sculley et al., Google, NIPS (2015) Intermediate

    "Only a tiny fraction of real-world ML systems is composed of the ML code." The paper that gave the industry its vocabulary for entanglement, feedback loops and pipeline jungles. Free PDF.

  • Scikit-learn: Machine Learning in Python — Pedregosa et al., JMLR (2011) Intermediate

    The design paper for the library you'll actually use — worth reading for where the fit/transform/predict API came from and why it's shaped that way.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • MLU-Explain — Amazon Machine Learning University Beginner

    Interactive visual essays on the core ideas — bias-variance, cross-validation, decision trees, random forest, ROC/AUC. Drag the sliders until the concept clicks.

  • Rules of Machine Learning: Best Practices for ML Engineering — Martin Zinkevich, Google Intermediate

    43 hard-won rules, in order, starting with "don't be afraid to launch a product without machine learning". The most useful thing to read before your first real project.

  • Common Pitfalls and Recommended Practices — scikit-learn documentation Intermediate

    Data leakage, preprocessing outside the cross-validation loop, misused random state — the specific mistakes that silently inflate your scores, with code showing right and wrong.

  • MLOps: Continuous delivery and automation pipelines in machine learning — Google Cloud Architecture Center Advanced

    The reference description of MLOps maturity levels 0 → 1 → 2, from manual notebooks to automated retraining and CI/CD. Vendor-hosted but largely vendor-neutral.

🎥 Videos & Talks

  • Machine Learning (playlist) — StatQuest with Josh Starmer Beginner

    Every algorithm and evaluation idea broken into a short, cheerful, unusually clear video. The standard rescue when a concept refuses to land.

  • Stanford CS229: Machine Learning — Andrew Ng, Stanford (Autumn 2018) Advanced

    The full graduate lectures, free, with the derivations left in. Course notes and problem sets are public at cs229.stanford.edu.

  • Learning From Data — Yaser S. Abu-Mostafa, Caltech Advanced

    18 free lectures built around one question: when is learning even possible? The clearest lecture treatment of VC dimension, overfitting and regularisation you'll find.

🎓 Courses & Tutorials

  • Machine Learning Crash Course — Google Beginner

    Free, self-paced, and heavy on interactive widgets and short exercises. The fastest route from nothing to training and evaluating your first models.

  • Kaggle Learn: Intro to Machine Learning — Kaggle Beginner

    A few hours, entirely in the browser, entirely hands-on. Follow it with Intermediate ML and Feature Engineering.

  • Machine Learning Specialization — Andrew Ng, Stanford & DeepLearning.AI (Coursera) Beginner

    The 2022 rebuild of the course that taught a generation this subject, now in Python. Paid subscription with financial aid available; the same ground is covered free by CS229 above.

  • scikit-learn User Guide — scikit-learn developers Intermediate

    Not just API docs — a genuine textbook on each model family, with the assumptions and failure modes stated. The page you'll keep open for years.

  • Made With ML — Goku Mohandas Intermediate

    A free, code-first course on taking one project all the way to production: design, data, testing, reproducibility, deployment, monitoring. Paid live cohorts exist; the written course doesn't need them.

  • Machine Learning Engineering for Production (MLOps) — DeepLearning.AI (Coursera) Intermediate

    Four courses on scoping, data-centric development, and deployment lifecycle — the structured counterpart to Made With ML. Paid subscription, financial aid available.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • scikit-learn Examples Gallery — runnable examples for every estimator

    Hundreds of short, plotted scripts. The quickest way to see how a model behaves is to find it here and change the numbers.

  • Kaggle — datasets, competitions and free hosted notebooks

    Real datasets to practise on and public solutions to read afterwards — the comparison against a strong baseline is where most of the learning happens.

  • Seeing Theory — Brown University Beginner

    Animated, interactive probability and statistics — distributions, estimation, regression. Fill the gaps underneath everything above.

🔗 Further Reading / Going Deeper

  • Interpretable Machine Learning — Christoph Molnar Intermediate

    Free online book on getting an answer out of a model, not just a prediction: partial dependence, permutation importance, LIME, SHAP, and where each one misleads.

  • Applying ML — Eugene Yan Intermediate

    Curated papers, company write-ups and practitioner interviews about ML that actually shipped. Useful antidote to tutorial-shaped problems.

  • Mathematics for Machine Learning — Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Cambridge University Press (2020) Intermediate

    Free PDF covering exactly the linear algebra, calculus, probability and optimisation the other books assume — and no more.

  • Probabilistic Machine Learning: An Introduction — Kevin P. Murphy, MIT Press (2022) Advanced

    The modern encyclopedic treatment, unified under a probabilistic view. Free draft PDF, with a second volume on advanced topics. Where to go when you want everything.

  • Transformers (Deep Learning) — on this site

    Where to go next when the data stops being tabular — text, images, audio — and hand-built features give way to learned representations.

Transformers

Last updated: 2026-07-30

The Transformer is a neural network architecture, introduced in 2017, that processes sequences using self-attention instead of the recurrence or convolutions earlier models relied on. Attention lets every token in a sequence weigh every other token directly, so the whole sequence is processed in parallel rather than one step at a time — which is exactly what made it worth throwing enormous amounts of compute at.

Transformers are the backbone of essentially every modern large language model, and they have spread well past text into vision, audio, code and protein structure. The core idea has barely changed since 2017, but the working architecture has: rotary position embeddings, mixture-of-experts layers, and IO-aware attention kernels are all standard now. This page follows that arc — get the mechanism first, then see what a 2026 model actually does differently.

Good to know first: basic neural networks and backpropagation, a little linear algebra (vectors, matrices, dot products), and some Python/PyTorch if you want to follow the hands-on resources.

📚 Books

  • Hands-On Large Language Models — Jay Alammar & Maarten Grootendorst, O'Reilly (2024) Beginner

    Over 275 custom diagrams from the author of The Illustrated Transformer — the gentlest path from "what is attention" to working with real models. Paid.

  • Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch) — Sebastian Raschka, Manning (2024) Intermediate

    Codes a GPT-style transformer step by step in PyTorch with no LLM libraries. Paid, but all the code is free at rasbt/LLMs-from-scratch.

  • Understanding Deep Learning — Simon J. D. Prince, MIT Press (2023) Intermediate

    Free PDF from the author's site; chapter 12 derives the transformer with unusually clear figures, and the notebooks let you poke at each piece.

  • Speech and Language Processing (3rd ed., draft) — Dan Jurafsky & James H. Martin Advanced

    The free, authoritative NLP textbook. Its transformer and LLM chapters are the ones to read when you want the math stated properly.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • Attention Is All You Need — Vaswani et al., NeurIPS (2017) Advanced

    The paper that introduced the Transformer. Short, and much easier once you have the intuition from the explainers below.

  • BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers — Devlin et al., NAACL (2019) Advanced

    The encoder branch of the family, and the paper that established pre-train-then-fine-tune as the default recipe. Still the right model class for embeddings and classification.

  • Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3) — Brown et al., NeurIPS (2020) Advanced

    Where in-context learning showed up: scale the decoder far enough and the model starts doing tasks from examples in the prompt, with no weight updates.

  • RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding — Su et al. (2021) Advanced

    RoPE replaced the original sinusoidal position encoding almost everywhere — read it to understand why modern models extend to long contexts as gracefully as they do.

  • FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention with IO-Awareness — Dao et al., NeurIPS (2022) Advanced

    Same attention, reorganized around GPU memory hierarchy. The clearest example of how hardware-aware engineering, not new math, unlocked long contexts.

  • Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Chinchilla) — Hoffmann et al., DeepMind (2022) Advanced

    Showed the field had been training models far too large on far too little data. The scaling-law result that still shapes how training budgets get spent.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • The Illustrated Transformer — Jay Alammar Beginner

    The most-read visual walkthrough of the architecture, and still the best first thing to read.

  • Transformers from Scratch — Peter Bloem, VU Amsterdam Intermediate

    Builds self-attention up from a plain weighted average, one honest step at a time — the explainer that makes the equations feel inevitable rather than arbitrary.

  • The Annotated Transformer — Harvard NLP Intermediate

    The original paper reproduced line by line, with a working PyTorch implementation interleaved into the text.

  • A Visual Guide to Mixture of Experts (MoE) — Maarten Grootendorst (2024) Intermediate

    Fifty-plus visualizations of experts, routers and load balancing — the sparse-layer design that most frontier models now use to grow parameters without growing inference cost.

  • The Big LLM Architecture Comparison — Sebastian Raschka, Ahead of AI (2025, updated since) Advanced

    Side-by-side teardown of recent open models — MoE layouts, latent attention, sliding windows, norm placement. The fastest way to see what actually changed since 2017.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • Transformer Explainer — Polo Club, Georgia Tech Beginner

    Runs a real GPT-2 in your browser: type a sentence and watch attention weights and next-token probabilities change as you edit it.

  • LLM Visualization — Brendan Bycroft Beginner

    A 3D walkthrough of every matrix in a working GPT, guided token by token. The best answer to "but what are the actual shapes?"

  • nanoGPT — Andrej Karpathy Intermediate

    A GPT you can read in an evening — ~300 lines of model, ~300 of training loop, and it really reproduces GPT-2. Karpathy now points people to his successor repo, nanochat, for the full ChatGPT-style pipeline.

  • TransformerLens — Neel Nanda and contributors Advanced

    Load a few thousand open models and read or patch their internal activations mid-forward-pass. The standard toolkit for doing interpretability yourself.

    Why serving is memory-bandwidth-bound, what the KV cache costs you, and how to estimate latency with arithmetic instead of benchmarks.

    A seminar series where the researchers behind each advance present it themselves — the closest thing to a running commentary on the field.

Generative AI (Beyond LLMs)

Last updated: 2026-09-25

A language model is trained to predict the next token. The models on this page are trained to do something different: learn the distribution that a pile of images, sounds or video frames was drawn from, then sample a new one out of it. Same deep-learning toolbox, genuinely different problem — there is no obvious "next item" in a picture, so instead of predicting forward you learn to turn noise into structure. Four families have answered that in turn: variational autoencoders, GANs, autoregressive models over discrete tokens, and diffusion models.

Diffusion won the last round. It displaced GANs on image quality and stability around 2021, underpins most text-to-image, text-to-video and text-to-audio generation, and is converging with flow matching into a single framework — though autoregressive models came back in 2025, when OpenAI's native image generation in GPT-4o turned out to be one. That convergence is what makes the topic worth studying as a whole rather than one modality at a time: the same denoising objective, guidance trick and sampler design reappear whether the output is a photograph, a cello line or a 3D scene.

Good to know first: comfortable Python, and enough neural-network familiarity to know what a layer, a loss and a gradient are. Be warned that the maths runs heavier here than for language models — expectations, KL divergence and a little stochastic calculus all show up. Prince and Murphy below build that groundwork from scratch, for free.

📚 Books

  • Generative Deep Learning (2nd ed.) — David Foster, O'Reilly (2023) Beginner

    The gentlest way in: VAEs, GANs, diffusion and music generation each built up and coded. Paid, but every notebook is free at davidADSP/Generative_Deep_Learning_2nd_Edition.

  • Hands-On Generative AI with Transformers and Diffusion Models — Sanseviero, Cuenca, Passos & Whitaker, O'Reilly (2024) Intermediate

    Written by four of the people behind Hugging Face's diffusion stack, and the most current book here on actually shipping image and audio generation. Paid.

  • Understanding Deep Learning — Simon J.D. Prince, MIT Press (2023) Intermediate

    Free PDF with a clean chapter per family — GANs, normalizing flows, VAEs, diffusion — and the best diagrams of any textbook on this list.

  • Probabilistic Machine Learning: Advanced Topics — Kevin P. Murphy, MIT Press (2023) Advanced

    Free PDF, and the reference to reach for when you want generative models placed rigorously inside probability theory rather than explained by analogy.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes — Kingma & Welling, ICLR (2014) Advanced

    The VAE, and the paper that made "learn a latent space you can sample from" a practical proposition. The reparameterisation trick here is reused everywhere below.

  • Generative Adversarial Networks — Goodfellow et al., NeurIPS (2014) Advanced

    Two networks in a minimax game — the idea that dominated generative imagery for six years. Read it for the framing even though diffusion has since overtaken it.

  • A Style-Based Generator Architecture for Generative Adversarial Networks — Karras, Laine & Aila, CVPR (2019) Advanced

    StyleGAN — the high-water mark of GAN image quality, and the origin of the disentangled latent space behind all those "this person does not exist" demos.

  • Neural Discrete Representation Learning — van den Oord, Vinyals & Kavukcuoglu, NeurIPS (2017) Advanced

    VQ-VAE: compress continuous data into discrete tokens. This is the trick that lets a transformer generate images and audio at all, and it powers modern audio codecs.

  • Zero-Shot Text-to-Image Generation (DALL·E) — Ramesh et al., OpenAI / ICML (2021) Advanced

    The autoregressive route to images: turn pictures into discrete tokens, then train one transformer over text and image tokens together. VAR (NeurIPS 2024) made the approach competitive again by predicting coarse-to-fine scales instead of one token at a time.

  • Addendum to GPT-4o System Card: Native image generation — OpenAI (2025) Intermediate

    Thin on architecture but explicit on the fact that matters here: unlike diffusion-based DALL·E 2 and 3, 4o image generation is autoregressive. Mostly a safety document, so also a look at the provenance and misuse controls shipped with it.

  • Deep Unsupervised Learning using Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics — Sohl-Dickstein et al., ICML (2015) Advanced

    The original diffusion paper, five years before anyone noticed. Destroy structure with noise, learn to reverse it — the whole idea is already here.

  • Generative Modeling by Estimating Gradients of the Data Distribution — Song & Ermon, NeurIPS (2019) Advanced

    Score matching plus annealed Langevin dynamics — the other half of the diffusion story, arrived at independently. Read it alongside DDPM to see two routes to one method.

  • Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models — Ho, Jain & Abbeel, NeurIPS (2020) Advanced

    The paper that made diffusion work, by simplifying the loss to plain noise prediction. If you read only one paper on this page, read this one.

  • Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models — Song, Meng & Ermon, ICLR (2021) Advanced

    DDIM cut sampling from a thousand steps to a few dozen and made the latent space interpolable — the first reason diffusion became usable interactively.

  • Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations — Song et al., ICLR (2021) Advanced

    Unifies DDPM and score matching as discretisations of one SDE. This is the paper that turns a bag of tricks into a theory you can reason about.

  • Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance — Ho & Salimans (2022) Intermediate

    Short, and responsible for the single knob you actually turn when prompting an image model. Explains what "guidance scale" is really doing.

  • Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models — Karras et al., NeurIPS (2022) Advanced

    Strips the accumulated notation and separates the real design choices — noise schedule, preconditioning, sampler. The clearest paper in the whole literature.

  • Flow Matching for Generative Modeling — Lipman et al., ICLR (2023) Advanced

    Learn a velocity field that transports noise to data along straight paths. Simpler than diffusion, and where the newest image and video models have moved.

  • Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision — Radford et al., OpenAI / ICML (2021) Intermediate

    CLIP put images and text in one embedding space. Not generative itself, but it is the component that made "type a prompt, get a picture" possible.

  • High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models — Rombach et al., CVPR (2022) Advanced

    Run diffusion in a compressed latent space instead of on pixels. This is Stable Diffusion, and the reason image generation fits on a consumer GPU.

  • Scalable Diffusion Models with Transformers — Peebles & Xie, ICCV (2023) Advanced

    Replaces the U-Net with a transformer and shows diffusion obeys familiar scaling laws. The DiT backbone behind Sora and most current video models.

  • Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis (Stable Diffusion 3) — Esser et al., Stability AI / ICML (2024) Advanced

    Rectified flow (straight-line flow matching) plus a transformer that keeps separate weights for text and image tokens, in one production model — the convergence described above, backed by a large-scale study of which choices matter.

  • Adding Conditional Control to Text-to-Image Diffusion Models — Zhang, Rao & Agrawala, ICCV (2023) Intermediate

    ControlNet — condition generation on a pose, depth map or scribble, so you get composition control instead of prompt roulette. Immediately practical.

  • WaveNet: A Generative Model for Raw Audio — van den Oord et al., DeepMind (2016) Intermediate

    Generating audio one sample at a time was supposed to be impossible. This is where neural speech synthesis stopped sounding robotic.

  • Simple and Controllable Music Generation — Copet et al., Meta / NeurIPS (2023) Intermediate

    MusicGen: one transformer over EnCodec tokens, conditioned on text or a melody. The clearest illustration of why discrete audio tokens matter.

  • Neural Codec Language Models are Zero-Shot Text to Speech Synthesizers (VALL-E) — Wang et al., Microsoft (2023) Intermediate

    Speech synthesis recast as language modelling over audio-codec tokens: three seconds of someone's voice is enough to continue in it. WaveNet's modern successor — and why voice cloning became a real misuse risk.

  • Video Diffusion Models — Ho et al., Google / NeurIPS (2022) Advanced

    Extends diffusion to time with a 3D U-Net and factorised space-time attention. The starting point for every text-to-video system that followed.

  • Movie Gen: A Cast of Media Foundation Models — Polyak et al., Meta (2024) Advanced

    A 30B-parameter transformer generating 16-second 1080p video with synchronised audio, with the architecture, training objective, data curation, evaluation and parallelism all written up — far more detail than any closed video model has published.

  • NeRF: Representing Scenes as Neural Radiance Fields for View Synthesis — Mildenhall et al., ECCV (2020) Intermediate

    Strictly this reconstructs a scene from photos rather than generating one — but it defines the 3D representation that generative text-to-3D builds on. The project page has the videos that make it click.

  • Instant Neural Graphics Primitives with a Multiresolution Hash Encoding — Müller et al., NVIDIA / SIGGRAPH (2022) Advanced

    Took NeRF training from hours to seconds with a hash-grid encoding. The engineering paper that made neural rendering practical to experiment with.

  • 3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering — Kerbl et al., INRIA / SIGGRAPH (2023) Advanced

    Drops neural fields for millions of explicit 3D Gaussians and hits real-time rendering. Also reconstruction, and it has largely displaced NeRF in practice — see the project page.

  • DreamFusion: Text-to-3D using 2D Diffusion — Poole et al., Google / ICLR (2023) Advanced

    Genuinely generative 3D with no 3D training data — distil a 2D image model into a NeRF via score distillation sampling. Results on the project page.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • The Illustrated Stable Diffusion — Jay Alammar Beginner

    Picture by picture through text encoder, U-Net and decoder, with no maths required. Start here before any paper above.

  • What are Diffusion Models? — Lilian Weng, Lil'Log Intermediate

    The canonical written explainer — every derivation spelled out, and kept updated through guidance, latent diffusion and consistency models.

  • The Annotated Diffusion Model — Rogge & Rasul, Hugging Face Intermediate

    DDPM reimplemented in PyTorch line by line beside the equations. The fastest way to convert reading into working code.

  • From GAN to WGAN — Lilian Weng, Lil'Log Advanced

    Why GANs are so hard to train — mode collapse, vanishing gradients — and how Wasserstein distance addresses it. The best single post on GAN failure modes.

  • Generative Modeling by Estimating Gradients of the Data Distribution — Yang Song Advanced

    The score-based view written by the author of the SDE paper, with animations. Read this and the SDE paper stops being intimidating.

  • Perspectives on diffusion — Sander Dieleman Advanced

    Diffusion models as autoencoders, latent variable models, score predictors, SDE solvers and flows — all at once. Read it once the mechanics are familiar.

  • Diffusion Models for Video Generation — Lilian Weng, Lil'Log (2024) Advanced

    What actually changes when you add a time axis — temporal consistency, joint training, and adapting a pretrained image model to video.

🎓 Courses & Tutorials

  • How Diffusion Models Work — Sharon Zhou, DeepLearning.AI Beginner

    Free, about an hour, and you build a small diffusion model end to end in notebooks. The cheapest useful first step.

  • Hugging Face Diffusion Models Course — Hugging Face Intermediate

    Free and code-first: theory, then notebooks covering fine-tuning, guidance and Stable Diffusion with the library everyone actually uses.

  • Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) Specialization — Sharon Zhou et al., DeepLearning.AI Intermediate

    Three courses building GANs in PyTorch, including evaluation and bias. Paid for the certificate, free to audit — still the most structured GAN treatment.

  • From Deep Learning Foundations to Stable Diffusion — Jeremy Howard et al., fast.ai Advanced

    Thirty hours that rebuild Stable Diffusion from matrix multiplication upward, made with Stability.ai and Hugging Face. Free, and the deepest hands-on option here.

  • Stanford CS236: Deep Generative Models — Stefano Ermon, Stanford Advanced

    Free lecture notes covering every family with the probability done properly. The academic spine under everything else on this page.

  • Berkeley CS294-158: Deep Unsupervised Learning — Pieter Abbeel et al., UC Berkeley (Spring 2024) Advanced

    Slides and lecture videos free, taught partly by DDPM's own authors. Go here when you want research-level depth across autoregressive, flow and diffusion models.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • Diffusion Explainer — Georgia Tech Polo Club

    Watch a real Stable Diffusion run in the browser, stepping through the prompt embedding and each denoising pass. Change the prompt and see what moves.

  • GAN Lab — Georgia Tech Polo Club

    Train a GAN in your browser and watch generator and discriminator fight in 2D. Mode collapse stops being an abstraction once you've caused it.

  • 🤗 Diffusers — Hugging Face

    The standard library for image, video and audio diffusion in PyTorch — pretrained pipelines for inference, swappable schedulers, and training scripts.

  • ComfyUI — node-based generation workflows

    Wire up samplers, ControlNets and upscalers as a visual graph. Makes the pipeline structure obvious in a way a one-line API call hides.

  • AudioCraft — Meta (MusicGen, AudioGen, EnCodec)

    Inference and training code for text-to-music and text-to-sound, plus the EnCodec tokenizer underneath. Code is MIT; model weights are non-commercial.

🔗 Further Reading / Going Deeper

  • Diffusion Models: A Comprehensive Survey of Methods and Applications — Yang et al. (2022, revised since)

    A structured map of hundreds of papers, sorted by technique and application. Use it to find whatever this page had to leave out.

  • Art and the science of generative AI: A deeper dive — Epstein et al. (2023)

    On training-data consent, authorship and what generative imagery does to creative work. Free extended version; the shorter original is paywalled in Science.

  • GANs Trained by a Two Time-Scale Update Rule Converge to a Local Nash Equilibrium — Heusel et al., NeurIPS (2017)

    Best known for introducing the Fréchet Inception Distance — the image-quality number reported by nearly every paper on this page. Read it to know what an FID score does and doesn't measure.

  • C2PA — Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity — open technical standard

    Signed "content credentials" that record how a piece of media was made and edited — the industry's main answer to telling generated images from captured ones, and what the provenance controls above plug into.

  • Large Language Models — on this site

    The other half of generative AI: next-token prediction, alignment, prompting, retrieval and agents.

  • Transformers — on this site

    Worth reading alongside DiT above — the architecture has quietly taken over diffusion backbones too.

  • Deep Learning — on this site

    The shared foundations: backpropagation, convolutional networks and the autoencoders that the VAE and U-Net designs on this page grew out of.

  • Machine Learning — on this site

    The ground underneath all of this: fitting models, honest evaluation, and why generalisation is the only question that matters.

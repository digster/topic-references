Decentralized Finance (DeFi)

Last updated: 2026-08-01

DeFi is what happens when you rebuild financial primitives — exchange, lending, margin, derivatives — as public smart contracts instead of companies. An automated market maker replaces the order book with a formula over two token reserves; a lending pool replaces underwriting with over-collateralisation and automatic liquidation; an oracle pipes in the outside prices all of it depends on. Anyone can call any of it, and every protocol can call every other, which is where the phrase "money legos" comes from.

It matters because it is the only part of crypto operating at real scale with real mechanisms worth studying, and because composability turns out to cut both ways: the same property that lets protocols stack lets an attacker chain them together inside a single transaction. Flash loans, oracle manipulation and MEV are not edge cases here, they are the native failure modes. The resources below cover the mechanism design and the exploits as one subject, because that is what they are.

Good to know first: smart contracts and how a transaction executes (see the Ethereum page below), plus enough finance vocabulary to be comfortable with collateral, leverage, liquidity and interest rates.

📚 Books

  • DeFi and the Future of Finance — Campbell R. Harvey, Ashwin Ramachandran & Joey Santoro, Wiley (2021) Intermediate

    The one book on this that a finance academic wrote — problem statement, mechanisms, then risks, without promotion. Paid; the authors' free companion course is Decentralized Finance Infrastructure on Coursera.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • Uniswap v2 Core — Adams, Zinsmeister & Robinson (2020) Intermediate

    The constant-product AMM specified properly. Short, and the single most load-bearing design in DeFi — read it before any explainer about "liquidity pools".

  • Uniswap v3 Core — Adams, Zinsmeister, Salem, Keefer & Robinson (2021) Advanced

    Concentrated liquidity: letting providers choose a price range, which turns a passive pool into an active position. The maths is heavier and worth the effort.

  • SoK: Decentralized Finance (DeFi) — Werner, Perez, Gudgeon, Klages-Mundt, Harz & Knottenbelt (2021) Advanced

    A systematisation of the whole field — protocol categories, primitives, attack surface — with a proper bibliography. The best single entry point to the literature.

  • Flash Boys 2.0: Frontrunning, Transaction Reordering, and Consensus Instability — Daian et al., IEEE S&P (2020) Advanced

    The paper that named MEV and showed arbitrage bots were bidding gas against each other to reorder your trades. It reframed block production as an auction, and the whole field followed.

  • Attacking the DeFi Ecosystem with Flash Loans for Fun and Profit — Qin, Zhou, Livshits & Gervais (2021) Advanced

    Dissects two real flash-loan attacks and then optimises them, showing the attacker could have taken far more. The clearest demonstration of why uncollateralised capital breaks assumptions.

  • An Empirical Study of DeFi Liquidations — Qin, Zhou, Gamito, Jovanovic & Gervais (2021) Advanced

    Measures what liquidations actually cost borrowers across the major lending protocols, and shows the designs over-punish. Rare quantitative work on a mechanism everyone assumes is fine.

  • Clockwork Finance: Automated Analysis of Economic Security in Smart Contracts — Babel, Daian, Kelkar & Juels (2023) Advanced

    Formal framework for reasoning about composed protocols, and it finds attacks automatically. Where DeFi security stops being pattern-matching and starts being verification.

  • DeFi risks and the decentralisation illusion — Aramonte, Huang & Schrimpf, BIS Quarterly Review (2021) Intermediate

    The central-bank argument that governance tokens and admin keys mean real control is concentrated regardless of the label. Short, and the criticism most worth having an answer to.

  • DeFi: Transformative Potential & Associated Risks — Carapella et al., Federal Reserve FEDS (2022) Intermediate

    A neutral regulator's map of the stack and its risks — leverage, liquidity mismatch, operational fragility. Useful for how supervisors see this, which increasingly shapes what ships.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • Decentralized Finance (DeFi) — ethereum.org Beginner

    The plain-language overview of what each category of protocol does, with the traditional-finance equivalent named next to it. The right first read.

  • Impermanent Loss Explained — Finematics Beginner

    The most misunderstood concept in DeFi, worked through with numbers: why providing liquidity can lose against simply holding the two tokens.

  • Uniswap v3 Math Primer — Uniswap Labs Advanced

    The bridge between the v3 whitepaper and the code — ticks, square-root prices, and why the implementation looks nothing like the equations at first.

  • Maximal Extractable Value (MEV) — ethereum.org Intermediate

    The maintained explanation of sandwich attacks, backrunning, proposer-builder separation and why MEV is a consensus-layer problem, not just a trading one.

  • Decoding MEV: Past, Present, Future — Finematics Intermediate

    How extraction evolved from gas-price wars into a structured supply chain of searchers, builders and relays. Good narrative context for the Flash Boys 2.0 paper.

  • TWAMM — Dave White, Dan Robinson & Hayden Adams, Paradigm (2021) Advanced

    A worked example of DeFi mechanism design being invented in public: an AMM that executes one enormous order gradually. Read it for the method as much as the design.

  • rekt.news — rekt Intermediate

    Post-mortems of hacks and rug pulls, ranked by amount lost, written with contempt and technical accuracy in equal measure. The leaderboard alone is an education.

🎓 Courses & Tutorials

  • Decentralized Finance MOOC — UC Berkeley RDI Advanced

    Free lectures from the researchers who wrote much of the literature above — mechanism design, oracles, MEV, security and regulation. The most rigorous course available.

  • Decentralized Finance Infrastructure — Campbell Harvey, Duke University (Coursera) Intermediate

    Free to audit, and the gentler on-ramp of the two: the plumbing explained by a finance professor rather than a cryptographer.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • DefiLlama — DefiLlama Beginner

    Open, ad-free dashboards of value locked, fees and revenue across every chain and protocol. The default place to check whether something is actually used.

  • Uniswap Documentation — Uniswap Labs Intermediate

    Protocol concepts alongside the contract interfaces and SDKs. The reference for building against the AMM the whitepapers describe.

  • Aave Documentation — Aave Intermediate

    How a large lending market is actually parameterised: interest-rate curves, health factors, liquidation thresholds and flash loans, all documented.

  • Compound Documentation — Compound Intermediate

    The other canonical lending design, worth reading beside Aave — the differences in collateral and rate models are where the interesting trade-offs live.

  • MakerDAO Documentation — MakerDAO Advanced

    The technical documentation for the original crypto-collateralised stablecoin — vaults, the debt auction, the emergency shutdown. Dense but the definitive worked example.

  • Chainlink Documentation — Chainlink Intermediate

    How off-chain prices get on-chain, which is the dependency almost every exploit above ends up abusing. Read the data-feed and VRF sections at minimum.

  • Flashbots Documentation — Flashbots Advanced

    The infrastructure that turned MEV from a gas auction into a private orderflow market — bundles, builders, relays and MEV-Boost, from the people who built it.

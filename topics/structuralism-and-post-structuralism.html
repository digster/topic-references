Structuralism and Post-Structuralism

Last updated: 2026-09-14

Structuralism is the claim that meaning comes from systems of relations rather than from things in themselves: a word signifies not because it names something in the world but because it differs from the other words around it. Ferdinand de Saussure made that argument about language, and a generation of French thinkers then ran the same move on everything else — Claude Lévi-Strauss on kinship and myth, Roland Barthes on advertising and wrestling, Jacques Lacan on the unconscious — always looking past the surface content for the underlying system that produces it.

Post-structuralism is what happened when that method was turned on itself. If meaning is only difference, it never fully settles, and the stable structures the first generation claimed to have discovered start to look like effects of language, history and power instead: Jacques Derrida showed that the system cannot close, and Michel Foucault asked whose institutions decide what counts as knowledge in the first place. Between them the two movements changed how the humanities read anything at all, and they are the source of a lot of now-loose vocabulary — the signifier, discourse, deconstruction, the death of the author — that is worth learning precisely.

Good to know first: nothing formal — but pick a novel, film or advert you know well and test each reading against it, because these theories are only legible in use. The later primary texts assume some Hegel, Marx, Freud, Husserl and Heidegger; the introductions below supply enough of that to keep going. Translation matters here, so the editions are named where it does.

📚 Books

  • Literary Theory: A Very Short Introduction (2nd ed.) — Jonathan Culler, Oxford University Press (2011) Beginner

    The best place to start: ~150 pages organised around problems rather than schools, so you meet structuralist and post-structuralist arguments doing actual work. Paid, and cheap.

  • Poststructuralism: A Very Short Introduction (2nd ed.) — Catherine Belsey, Oxford University Press (2022) Beginner

    The companion volume, and unusually honest about the resistance and misreadings the label attracts. The second edition adds Kofman and Kristeva to the usual Barthes–Foucault–Derrida trio. Paid.

  • Literary Theory: An Introduction (Anniversary ed.) — Terry Eagleton, University of Minnesota Press (2008) Beginner

    The survey that taught the English-speaking world this material; its structuralism and post-structuralism chapters are still the clearest narrative of how one became the other. Opinionated — Eagleton is a Marxist and says so. Paid.

  • Course in General Linguistics — Ferdinand de Saussure, reconstructed from students' notes (1916) Intermediate

    The source of everything downstream — signifier/signified, langue/parole, and the argument that language is a system of differences with no positive terms. Paid in this edition; the 1959 Baskin translation is free in full on the Internet Archive.

  • Structural Anthropology — Claude Lévi-Strauss, Basic Books (1963; French 1958) Intermediate

    Where Saussure's method leaves linguistics and becomes a way of reading kinship systems and myths. Paid; the pivotal second chapter is free at the Marxists Internet Archive.

  • Mythologies (Complete Edition) — Roland Barthes, trans. Howard & Lavers, Hill and Wang (2012; French 1957) Beginner

    Fifty-odd short essays reading wrestling, steak, soap powder and the Citroën DS as sign systems — the most enjoyable way into structuralism, and still the best demonstration of what the method buys you. Paid.

  • S/Z — Roland Barthes, trans. Richard Miller, Hill and Wang (1974; French 1970) Advanced

    Barthes breaking a thirty-page Balzac story into 561 fragments and five codes — and, in the doing, breaking structuralism itself. The single clearest place to watch the turn happen in one author. Paid.

  • Structuralist Poetics — Jonathan Culler, Routledge (1975, reissued 2002) Advanced

    The rigorous account of what a structuralist reading actually claims, and the origin of "literary competence" — the reader's internalised system that makes interpretation possible. Paid.

  • Of Grammatology (Fortieth Anniversary ed.) — Jacques Derrida, trans. Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak, Johns Hopkins University Press (2016; French 1967) Advanced

    The book that made deconstruction a method rather than a mood, arguing that Western thought has always quietly privileged speech over writing. Hard going; Spivak's preface and Butler's new introduction are the handrails. Paid — free excerpts here.

  • On Deconstruction: Theory and Criticism after Structuralism — Jonathan Culler, Cornell University Press (1982) Intermediate

    Read this next to Of Grammatology, not instead of it: the best available explanation of what Derrida is doing and why it follows from structuralism rather than rejecting it. Paid.

  • The Order of Things: An Archaeology of the Human Sciences — Michel Foucault, Routledge Classics (2001; French 1966) Advanced

    Foucault's case that each era has an underlying "episteme" deciding what can count as knowledge — structuralist in method, corrosive to structuralism's claim to stand outside history. Paid; start with the famous reading of Velázquez's Las Meninas.

  • History of Structuralism, Volume 1: The Rising Sign, 1945–1966 — François Dosse, University of Minnesota Press (1997) Advanced

    The intellectual history: who read whom, which journals and seminars carried it, and why Paris in particular. Read it after the theory, when you want to know how the movement actually happened. Paid.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • Lectures on General Linguistics (excerpts) — Ferdinand de Saussure (1916), Marxists Internet Archive Intermediate

    The load-bearing passages of the Course in twenty minutes, free — the arbitrariness of the sign and the synchronic/diachronic split. Read this before anything else on this page.

  • Structural Analysis in Linguistics and in Anthropology — Claude Lévi-Strauss, Structural Anthropology ch. II (1958) Intermediate

    The founding move of the whole movement, stated openly: linguistics has become a real science, so the other social sciences should borrow its method. Free.

  • Elements of Semiology (excerpts) — Roland Barthes (1964), Marxists Internet Archive Intermediate

    Barthes turning Saussure's linguistics into a general toolkit for reading non-verbal sign systems — clothes, food, cars. The method behind Mythologies, spelled out. Free.

  • The Death of the Author — Roland Barthes (1967), trans. Stephen Heath Beginner

    Six pages, enormously consequential, and the most-cited thing on this page: meaning belongs to the reader and the language, not to the writer's biography. Free PDF.

  • Structure, Sign and Play in the Discourse of the Human Sciences — Jacques Derrida (1966), trans. Alan Bass; edition typeset by Martin Paul Eve Advanced

    The hinge text of this entire topic — the lecture where Derrida points out that every structure needs a centre it cannot itself contain. Free, eleven pages, and worth several rereads.

  • Derrida delivering it: conference recordings, 21 October 1966 — Sheridan Libraries, Johns Hopkins University (digitised 2023) Advanced

    The actual tape from Baltimore, free, including the hostile Q&A that the published essay omits — his own teacher Jean Hyppolite among the perplexed. A rare chance to hear a field turn over in real time.

  • The Archaeology of Knowledge (excerpts) — Michel Foucault (1969), Marxists Internet Archive Advanced

    Foucault explaining his own method — "discourse", the statement, and why he refuses the structuralist label everyone kept applying to him. Free.

  • Ideology and Ideological State Apparatuses — Louis Althusser (1970), Marxists Internet Archive Advanced

    The structuralist reading of Marx, and the origin of "interpellation" — how institutions hail you into being a particular kind of subject. Free, and the bridge to most later cultural theory.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • Literary Theory — Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy Beginner

    The single best free overview: one long article that places structuralism and post-structuralism among the schools they argued with, rather than treating either in isolation.

  • Recordings and Transcriptions of "The Languages of Criticism and the Sciences of Man" — Mack Zalin, Sheridan Libraries, Johns Hopkins University (2023) Intermediate

    Short, and the clearest account of the four days in October 1966 when French theory arrived in America — including what the published proceedings left out.

  • Jacques Derrida — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy Advanced

    Rigorous and long, and the only free reference that reconstructs différance as an argument with premises rather than restating it as a slogan.

  • Derrida, Jacques — Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy Intermediate

    Gentler than the Stanford entry and better on the later ethical and political writing. Read it first if the Stanford one loses you.

  • Michel Foucault — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy Advanced

    Traces the move from archaeology to genealogy across the whole career, which is the thing most short summaries of Foucault get wrong.

  • Jacques Lacan — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy Advanced

    For the third leg of the tripod: Lacan reading Freud through Saussure, and why "the unconscious is structured like a language" is a technical claim rather than a flourish.

  • How To Deconstruct Almost Anything — Chip Morningstar (1993) Beginner

    A software engineer's hostile field report from a semiotics conference — funny, unfair in places, and a useful inoculation against mistaking the jargon for the argument. Read it after the Culler, not before.

🎥 Videos & Talks

  • Structuralism and Semiotics: WTF? Saussure, Lévi-Strauss, Barthes and Structuralism Explained — Tom Nicholas Beginner

    Twenty minutes, worked examples, no name-dropping — the fastest way to get the core idea into your head before you read anything.

  • Poststructuralism: WTF? Derrida, Deconstruction and Poststructuralist Theory Explained — Tom Nicholas Beginner

    The direct sequel, and it earns its place by explaining post-structuralism as a consequence of structuralism rather than a fashion that replaced it.

  • Understanding Derrida, Deconstruction & Of Grammatology — Then & Now Beginner

    A patient walk through the book most people give up on, with the speech/writing argument reconstructed step by step.

  • Foucault: Power, Knowledge and Post-structuralism — Michael Sugrue Intermediate

    A full university lecture, chalk-and-talk, from a professor who takes the ideas seriously enough to argue with them. The best single hour on Foucault online.

  • Derrida and Deconstruction — Peter Salmon — Verso Books Intermediate

    Derrida's biographer on the life and the politics, which is the context the primary texts strip out. Good once you know the concepts.

🎓 Courses & Tutorials

  • ENGL 300: Introduction to Theory of Literature — Paul H. Fry, Open Yale Courses Intermediate

    The best free course on this material anywhere: 26 filmed lectures with transcripts and a reading list. For this topic go straight to 8 (Semiotics and Structuralism), 9 (Linguistics and Literature), 10 and 11 (Deconstruction I and II).

  • Philosophize This! #115–#123: Structuralism to Foucault — Stephen West Beginner

    Free podcast episodes that run the arc in order — structuralism, myth, the post-modern turn, Derrida, then three on Foucault. Ideal for commutes; transcripts on the site.

  • The Modern and the Postmodern (Part 1) — Michael Roth, Wesleyan University, on Coursera Intermediate

    Puts the movement in its longer history, from Kant and Rousseau through to the post-modern turn. Free to audit; paid for the certificate.

  • Structuralism: Saussure and the Origins of Structuralism — Dr Paul-François Tremlett, The Open University, on MASSOLIT Beginner

    Short, tightly-scoped video lectures aimed at students meeting this for the first time. Subscription — sample the free lectures before paying.

🔗 Further Reading / Going Deeper

  • The Postmodern Condition: A Report on Knowledge — Jean-François Lyotard, University of Minnesota Press (1984; French 1979) Advanced

    Where post-structuralism becomes a diagnosis of the age — "incredulity toward metanarratives". Paid; free excerpts here.

  • A Thousand Plateaus: Capitalism and Schizophrenia — Gilles Deleuze & Félix Guattari, University of Minnesota Press (1987; French 1980) Advanced

    The wildest thing the movement produced — rhizomes against trees, a book designed to be read in any order. Paid, and genuinely difficult; save it for last.

  • Postmodernism — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy Intermediate

    Free, and useful mainly for drawing the line these three words keep blurring: what post-structuralism actually is, and what postmodernism adds on top.

  • Transgressing the Boundaries: Toward a Transformative Hermeneutics of Quantum Gravity — Alan Sokal, Social Text (1996), annotated on the author's own page Intermediate

    The hoax paper that a cultural-studies journal published in earnest, free and with Sokal's notes. The strongest case for the prosecution — read it alongside the theory, not instead of it.

  • Structuralism — Monoskop

    A free, heavily-linked bibliography of the movement and its figures — the place to go when you want the next thing by an author and don't know what it is.

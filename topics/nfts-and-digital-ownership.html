NFTs and Digital Ownership

Last updated: 2026-08-01

A non-fungible token is a smart contract entry that maps a unique ID to an owner's address. That is genuinely all it is — the standard, ERC-721, is about 20 lines of interface. What the token points at is a separate question, and usually the important one: most NFTs store a URL in the contract and the actual image somewhere else, so "owning" one means owning a ledger entry that references a file you don't control.

NFTs matter for two reasons that have nothing to do with the 2021 bubble. First, they are the clearest case study in the gap between what a technology guarantees and what buyers assumed it guaranteed — permanence, copyright, royalties, none of which come for free. Second, the subset that puts the artwork's generating code fully on-chain is a real and durable art form. This page covers the standards, the storage problem, the art, and what the market data showed afterwards.

Good to know first: smart contracts and token standards — an NFT is an ERC-20 with the fungibility removed, so the Ethereum page below is the natural prerequisite.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • EIP-721: Non-Fungible Token Standard — Entriken, Shirley, Evans & Sachs (2018) Intermediate

    The spec itself, and short enough to read in one sitting. The rationale section is unusually candid about what the authors deliberately left out — including any guarantee about the metadata.

  • EIP-1155: Multi Token Standard — Radomski, Cooke, Castonguay, Therien, Binet & Sandford (2018) Intermediate

    One contract holding many token types, fungible or not, with batch transfers. The standard games and marketplaces actually use once minting thousands of items gets expensive.

  • EIP-2981: NFT Royalty Standard — Burks, Morgan, Malone & Seibel (2020) Intermediate

    A way to *signal* a royalty, which marketplaces then chose whether to honour. The best short illustration that on-chain data is not the same as on-chain enforcement.

  • ERC-6551: Non-fungible Token Bound Accounts — Benet, Pandolfi, Chalkias et al. (2023) Advanced

    Gives every NFT its own smart-contract wallet, so a token can own assets itself. The most interesting recent extension, and a genuine capability rather than a market feature.

  • ERC-721 — Ethereum developer docs — ethereum.org Beginner

    The annotated walkthrough of the standard with a worked example contract. Read this alongside the EIP if the raw spec is too terse.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • Non-fungible tokens (NFTs) — ethereum.org Beginner

    The plain explanation of what the token does and doesn't confer, including the copyright point most coverage skipped entirely.

  • How NFTs Create Value — Steve Kaczynski & Scott Duke Kominers, Harvard Business Review (2021) Beginner

    The serious economic argument — that the value is in verifiable membership and community access, not the picture. Written by a market-design economist. Paywalled after a few free articles.

  • NFT Canon — a16z crypto Intermediate

    A curated reading list across technical foundations, creator economics, law and culture. Investor-published and therefore optimistic, but the links themselves are well chosen.

  • Soulbound — Vitalik Buterin (2022) Intermediate

    The argument for non-transferable tokens as credentials and reputation. The most influential attempt to point this technology at something other than speculation.

  • NFT Wash Trading and Money Laundering — Chainalysis (2022) Intermediate

    On-chain evidence that a meaningful share of reported volume was self-dealing. Essential context for any chart of NFT sales from that period.

  • Right Click Save — Right Click Save Intermediate

    Criticism and essays treating this as an art movement with a history, by writers who know both the art world and the technology. The antidote to both hype and dismissal.

  • Artnome — Jason Bailey Intermediate

    Long-form writing on generative and computational art that predates the NFT boom and takes the lineage seriously — Vera Molnár and Harold Cohen, not just 2021.

  • Beeple, Everydays: The First 5000 Days — Christie's (2021) Beginner

    The $69m auction lot that made this a mainstream story, still on Christie's site. The lot notes are a useful primary document of how the traditional art market framed it.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • Art Blocks — Art Blocks Beginner

    Generative art where the script lives on-chain and the output is produced at mint from the transaction hash. The strongest existing argument that the medium is real; the docs explain the mechanism.

  • IPFS Documentation — IPFS / Protocol Labs Intermediate

    Where most NFT metadata actually lives, and why content addressing is not the same as permanence — files vanish when nobody pins them.

  • Arweave — Arweave Intermediate

    The pay-once-store-forever alternative, with an endowment model instead of ongoing pinning. Worth understanding as the other answer to the storage problem.

  • OpenSea Developer Documentation — OpenSea Intermediate

    The de facto metadata conventions — how tokenURI should be shaped, what fields marketplaces read — which are convention rather than standard.

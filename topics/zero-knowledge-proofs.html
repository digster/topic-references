Zero-Knowledge Proofs

Last updated: 2026-08-01

A zero-knowledge proof lets one party convince another that a statement is true while revealing nothing beyond its truth — not the password, not the inputs, not the witness. The idea is from 1985 and sat mostly in theory for thirty years. What changed is succinctness: modern constructions (SNARKs and STARKs) produce proofs of a few hundred bytes that verify in milliseconds no matter how large the computation being proved was.

That property turns out to be more useful for scaling than for privacy. If a proof that a million transactions executed correctly is cheaper to check than the transactions are to re-run, a blockchain can verify work it never did — which is exactly what a ZK rollup is. The same machinery underpins private payments, anonymous credentials and proof of solvency. This page follows that arc: intuition first, then the constructions, then writing circuits yourself.

Good to know first: comfort with modular arithmetic and polynomials is enough for the explainers; the papers assume finite fields, elliptic curves and pairings. You can get a long way on intuition before you need any of it.

📚 Books

  • The MoonMath Manual to zk-SNARKs — Least Authority Intermediate

    Free, and unusual in actually teaching the prerequisite maths as it goes rather than assuming it. The bridge for developers who bounced off the papers.

  • Proofs, Arguments, and Zero-Knowledge — Justin Thaler (2023) Advanced

    The standard graduate text, free as a PDF from the author. Builds interactive proofs, sumcheck and modern arguments in the right order — the book to work through if you want to do this properly.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • On the Size of Pairing-based Non-interactive Arguments — Jens Groth, EUROCRYPT (2016) Advanced

    "Groth16" — three group elements, constant-size proofs, and still the smallest and fastest-to-verify SNARK in production. The cost is a per-circuit trusted setup.

  • PLONK: Permutations over Lagrange-bases for Oecumenical Noninteractive arguments of Knowledge — Gabizon, Williamson & Ciobotaru (2019) Advanced

    One universal setup for all circuits instead of one per circuit. That single change is why most modern proving stacks are PLONK-shaped rather than Groth16-shaped.

  • Scalable, transparent, and post-quantum secure computational integrity — Ben-Sasson, Bentov, Horesh & Riabzev (2018) Advanced

    The STARK paper: no trusted setup, hash-based so plausibly post-quantum, at the cost of much larger proofs. The other branch of the family tree.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • Zero-Knowledge Proofs — ethereum.org Beginner

    What the guarantee is, what the trade-offs between SNARKs and STARKs are, and where they're actually used. The right orientation before anything heavier.

  • zkIntro — zkintro.com Beginner

    A friendly, well-paced introduction aimed at developers, building intuition with worked examples before any algebra shows up.

  • Explaining SNARKs — Ariel Gabizon, Electric Coin Company Intermediate

    A seven-part series from homomorphic hidings through to pairings, by one of PLONK's authors. The clearest incremental path into the real construction.

  • Quadratic Arithmetic Programs: from Zero to Hero — Vitalik Buterin (2016) Intermediate

    How a program becomes an arithmetic circuit, then R1CS, then a polynomial identity. The single most important step to actually understand, and this is where most people finally get it.

  • Exploring Elliptic Curve Pairings — Vitalik Buterin (2017) Advanced

    The second instalment: what a pairing gives you that plain elliptic-curve arithmetic doesn't, explained without the algebraic geometry.

  • zk-SNARKs: Under the Hood — Vitalik Buterin (2017) Advanced

    The payoff post that assembles the previous two into a working scheme, including what the trusted setup's "toxic waste" actually is and why it matters.

  • An approximate introduction to how zk-SNARKs are possible — Vitalik Buterin (2021) Intermediate

    A later, self-contained rewrite that gets to the core idea faster than the 2016–17 series. Read this one first if you only read one.

  • Groth16 Explained — RareSkills Advanced

    Walks the whole pipeline — circuit to R1CS to QAP to the final pairing check — with the algebra written out. The best companion to Groth's paper.

  • Zero Knowledge Canon — a16z crypto Intermediate

    A large annotated reading list ordered from intuition to research frontier, with each entry's difficulty flagged. Useful as a map once you know what you're looking for.

🎓 Courses & Tutorials

  • ZKP MOOC — Boneh, Thaler, Kothapalli et al., UC Berkeley RDI Advanced

    A full free university course with lectures, slides and exercises from the researchers who wrote the field's standard references. The most rigorous free option.

  • 0xPARC Learning Resources — 0xPARC Intermediate

    Practitioner-oriented material on circuit programming and applications, including the halo2 and Circom course notes. Aimed at building rather than proving theorems.

  • Circom Documentation — iden3 Intermediate

    The circuit language most people learn first. Working through the tutorial is where constraints stop being abstract and start being something you get wrong.

  • Noir Documentation — Aztec Intermediate

    A Rust-like language that hides the constraint system behind ordinary control flow. The gentler on-ramp if Circom's arithmetisation is too raw.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • ZKDocs — Trail of Bits Advanced

    Precise, implementation-focused documentation of the primitives — Fiat-Shamir, sigma protocols, commitments — with the footguns called out. The reference for getting details right.

  • RISC Zero — RISC Zero Advanced

    A zkVM that proves ordinary Rust execution, so you can produce proofs without writing a circuit at all. The clearest demonstration of where this technology is heading.

  • SP1 Documentation — Succinct Advanced

    The other production zkVM, with good writing on proof aggregation and recursion. Worth reading beside RISC Zero for the design contrasts.

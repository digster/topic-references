Crypto Economics and DAOs

Last updated: 2026-08-01

Crypto economics is mechanism design with a compiler. Because a blockchain can hold funds and execute rules automatically, incentive schemes that economists could only write down can now be deployed and tested against people who want the money — mining rewards, staking penalties, fee markets, token emissions. A DAO is the same idea applied to the organisation itself: a treasury and a rulebook held in smart contracts, with decisions made by token holders rather than a board.

It matters because this is where crypto's claims are ultimately settled. Most protocol failures are not cryptographic — they are incentive failures: governance captured by whoever bought the most tokens, emissions that pay for mercenary liquidity, voters who rationally don't vote. The honest version of this subject includes the results that didn't work, and the resources below are chosen accordingly.

Good to know first: smart contracts, and enough game theory to be comfortable with incentives, equilibria and collusion. Some background in public-goods economics helps for the quadratic funding material.

📚 Books

  • Token Economy (3rd edition) — Shermin Voshmgir (2025) Beginner

    The broadest survey of token types and their economics — money, NFTs, DeFi, DAOs and purpose-driven tokens — readable free online, print editions paid. Written by an academic, not a promoter.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • A Flexible Design for Funding Public Goods — Vitalik Buterin, Zoë Hitzig & E. Glen Weyl (2019) Advanced

    The quadratic funding paper: match donations by the square of the sum of square roots, so the number of contributors matters more than their wealth. The most genuinely novel mechanism to come out of this space.

  • Decentralized Autonomous Organisations (DAOs) — ethereum.org Beginner

    The maintained reference: what a DAO is, how membership and voting are typically implemented, and the legal status question that has no clean answer yet.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • DAOs, DACs, DAs and More: An Incomplete Terminology Guide — Vitalik Buterin, Ethereum Foundation (2014) Beginner

    Where the vocabulary was set, two years before anyone built one. Worth reading to see how much of the original definition survived contact with reality.

  • DAOs are not corporations — Vitalik Buterin (2022) Intermediate

    Argues decentralised governance is the right choice for censorship resistance and *wrong* for efficiency, so the question is which decisions need which. The most useful framing on this page.

  • Moving beyond coin voting governance — Vitalik Buterin (2021) Intermediate

    A direct critique of one-token-one-vote by the person most associated with it — vote buying, whale dominance, and why bribery attacks are cheap. Read this before designing any governance.

  • Notes on Blockchain Governance — Vitalik Buterin (2017) Intermediate

    On why on-chain voting is not automatically more legitimate than rough consensus, and what "governance" is even supposed to be deciding.

  • DAO Canon — a16z crypto Intermediate

    A curated reading list across governance design, legal structure and operations, including the practical entity-wrapper material that is hard to find elsewhere.

  • WTF is Quadratic Funding? — Gitcoin Beginner

    An interactive explainer with sliders — move the contributions and watch the match change. The fastest way to understand the paper above without the algebra.

  • RadicalxChange — RadicalxChange Foundation Intermediate

    The wider mechanism-design movement quadratic voting and funding came out of — partial common ownership, plural voting, data dignity. The intellectual source, largely outside crypto.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • Tally Documentation — Tally Intermediate

    How on-chain governance is actually wired: Governor contracts, proposal lifecycles, timelocks, quorum and delegation. The concrete counterpart to the essays above.

  • Curve Documentation — Curve Advanced

    The vote-escrow model — lock tokens for up to four years to get voting power over emissions — and the whole bribe economy that grew around it. The most-copied and most-studied token design in DeFi.

  • Gitcoin — Gitcoin Beginner

    Quadratic funding running at scale for years, with published results and documented sybil-resistance problems. The largest live experiment in any mechanism on this page.

🔗 Further Reading / Going Deeper

  • Ethereum Research — Ethereum Foundation Advanced

    The economics categories cover fee markets, staking incentives and proposer-builder separation — mechanism design being argued out at protocol scale, in public.

  • Decentralized Finance (DeFi) — on this site

    Where these token models are deployed and stress-tested: the protocols whose emissions, treasuries and governance votes are the subject matter above.

  • Ethereum and Smart Contracts — on this site

    The code a DAO actually is — Governor contracts, timelocks and treasuries — and the security review any of it needs before it holds funds.

  • Blockchain and Cryptocurrency — on this site

    The original crypto-economic mechanism: block rewards and difficulty adjustment, which are the reason any of this is more than a thought experiment.

  • NFTs and Digital Ownership — on this site

    Non-fungible tokens as membership and credentials — token-gated governance, soulbound identity, and the royalty enforcement problem.

Blockchain and Cryptocurrency

Last updated: 2026-08-01

A blockchain is an append-only ledger that a group of mutually distrusting computers can agree on without anyone being in charge. The trick is not the "chain of blocks" — it's the consensus rule that makes rewriting history expensive: proof of work burns electricity, proof of stake burns capital. Bitcoin, described in a nine-page paper in 2008, was the first system to make this work at all, and everything else on this page descends from it.

It matters because it is the first credible answer to a very old question: how do you move value between strangers with no bank, court or government in the middle? Whether that answer is useful is genuinely contested — the same properties that make censorship resistance possible also make fraud irreversible, and the industry has an ugly track record. The resources below teach the mechanism first, then give both the builders' case and the critics' case honestly.

Good to know first: nothing formal, but a rough idea of what a cryptographic hash function and a digital signature do will make the consensus material click much faster. Basic economics helps for the money side.

📚 Books

  • Grokking Bitcoin — Kalle Rosenbaum, Manning (2019) Beginner

    Builds Bitcoin up from a spreadsheet of IOUs to the real protocol, one honest step at a time. The gentlest technical introduction that doesn't lie to you. Paid.

  • Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies — Narayanan, Bonneau, Felten, Miller & Goldfeder, Princeton (2016) Intermediate

    The academic textbook, free as a full draft PDF. Treats consensus, mining and anonymity as computer science rather than investment advice — still the best conceptual grounding.

  • Mastering Bitcoin (3rd edition) — Andreas M. Antonopoulos & David A. Harding, O'Reilly (2023) Intermediate

    The reference implementation-level book: keys, addresses, transactions, script, the mempool. Free to read in full on GitHub under CC BY-SA; paid in print.

  • Digital Gold — Nathaniel Popper, Harper (2015) Beginner

    A reporter's history of Bitcoin's first six years through the people involved. Read it for why the thing exists and who built it, not for how it works. Paid.

  • The Blocksize War — Jonathan Bier (2021) Intermediate

    A blow-by-blow account of the 2015–17 fight over Bitcoin's block size — the clearest case study anywhere of how a decentralised system actually makes decisions. Paid.

📄 Papers & Primary References

  • Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System — Satoshi Nakamoto (2008) Intermediate

    Nine pages that started all of it. Genuinely readable, and short enough that there's no excuse for taking anyone else's word for what it says.

  • Proof of Stake — Ethereum developer docs — ethereum.org Intermediate

    The maintained explanation of the other major consensus family: validators, staking, attestations, finality and slashing, with the trade-offs against proof of work stated plainly.

  • The Complete Satoshi — Nakamoto Institute Intermediate

    Every known email, forum post and code comment from Bitcoin's pseudonymous author, indexed and dated. Primary source material for the design decisions.

  • The Future Monetary System — Bank for International Settlements, Annual Economic Report (2022) Advanced

    The central bankers' technical critique — fragmentation, the scalability trilemma and why they think the structural flaws are inherent rather than fixable. Worth reading precisely because it argues the other side well.

✍️ Articles & Blog Posts

  • River Learn — River Financial Beginner

    A well-organised reference wiki covering terms, mechanics and history in short articles. Published by a Bitcoin company, so read the "why" pages with that in mind.

  • Stablecoins — ethereum.org Beginner

    The category that carries most real crypto payment volume: how fiat-backed, crypto-backed and algorithmic designs differ, and how each one breaks.

  • Some reflections on the Bitcoin block size war — Vitalik Buterin (2024) Intermediate

    Ethereum's founder reviewing Bier's book on Bitcoin's civil war, and drawing out what it says about governance in any decentralised system. A good companion to the book.

  • Bitcoin Is Worse Is Better — Gwern Branwen (2011) Advanced

    Why Bitcoin succeeded where decades of cleaner academic e-cash designs failed. The sharpest short essay on what was actually novel about it.

  • The Case Against Crypto — Stephen Diehl (2021) Beginner

    The best-argued short version of the sceptic's position: that the technology is a solution in search of a problem and the economics are negative-sum. He revisited it in The Case Against Crypto in 2025.

  • Web3 is Going Just Great — Molly White Beginner

    A running, sourced timeline of hacks, rug pulls and collapses with dollar figures attached. The single most efficient antidote to reading only the optimistic material.

  • Crypto Crime Report — Chainalysis (annual) Intermediate

    Actual numbers on illicit volume, scams, ransomware and stolen funds, from the firm that does the on-chain forensics. Useful for arguing about crypto crime with data instead of vibes.

  • Bitcoin Resources — Jameson Lopp Intermediate

    A long-maintained link directory covering everything from cryptography primers to node operation and self-custody. Where to go when you want more depth on one specific piece.

🎥 Videos & Talks

  • But how does bitcoin actually work? — 3Blue1Brown Beginner

    Derives the whole design from scratch by asking what a digital ledger between friends would need. If you watch only one thing on this page, watch this.

  • Lecture 1 — Intro to Crypto and Cryptocurrencies — Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies, Princeton Intermediate

    The opening lecture of the Princeton course that accompanies the textbook above — hash functions and digital signatures built up properly before any coins appear.

🎓 Courses & Tutorials

  • Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies — Princeton University, Coursera Intermediate

    The full course behind the textbook, free to audit. Eleven weeks from cryptographic primitives to mining economics, altcoins and the politics of the thing.

  • Bitcoin Developer Documentation — Bitcoin Project Advanced

    The protocol reference: transaction format, script, P2P messages, RPC. Where to go once you want to build against Bitcoin rather than read about it.

🔧 Tools & Interactive

  • Blockchain Demo — Anders Brownworth Beginner

    Type into a block, watch the hash go red, then watch every later block break. Five minutes here explains immutability better than any paragraph.

  • Bitcoin Wiki — community-maintained Intermediate

    The old community reference for opcodes, protocol details and historical trivia. Uneven and sometimes dated, but it has things documented nowhere else.

🔗 Further Reading / Going Deeper

  • Satoshi Nakamoto Institute — Nakamoto Institute Advanced

    The pre-history: Chaum's digital cash, Szabo's bit gold, Dai's b-money, the cypherpunk mailing lists. Bitcoin makes much more sense once you see what it was assembled from.

  • Ethereum and Smart Contracts — on this site

    The obvious next step: what happens when the ledger can also run arbitrary programs, and how you write them.

  • Decentralized Finance (DeFi) — on this site

    The largest thing actually built on this technology — exchanges, lending and stablecoins with no institution behind them.

  • NFTs and Digital Ownership — on this site

    The other headline use case: using a ledger to track unique items rather than fungible money, and what that did and didn't deliver.

  • Layer 2 and Scaling — on this site

    Where the throughput limits described above actually get addressed — rollups, data availability and the modular stack.

  • Crypto Economics and DAOs — on this site

    The incentive design underneath consensus, generalised: token models, governance, and what happens when a protocol tries to govern itself.

  • Zero-Knowledge Proofs — on this site

    The cryptography that lets a chain verify a computation it never ran — now the basis of both privacy and most scaling work.

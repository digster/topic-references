## Verdict: keep GA and GP on one page

GP is not a separate discipline from GAs. It is the same evolutionary loop with a different
representation: programs (expression trees) evolve instead of fixed-length strings.

- **Same algorithm.** Population, fitness, selection, crossover and mutation work the same way
  in both. Koza introduced GP as Holland's GA applied to LISP trees, and the page's own intro
  frames it that way.
- **Splitting would duplicate most of the page.** Eiben & Smith, Luke, No Free Lunch, NSGA-II,
  specification gaming, DEAP and pymoo apply to both. Two pages would either copy them or send
  readers back and forth.
- **GP alone would be thin.** The answer counted about 9 clearly GP-only resources: Poli's
  *Field Guide*, Koza 1989, Schmidt & Lipson, Cranmer's talk, the gplearn tutorial, PySR, TPOT,
  SRBench and Langdon's bibliography. Checked against the repo, Koza's 1992 book is GP-only too,
  so it is about 10 of 53 external rows. Still a weak page next to the others.
- **It fits the site's pattern.** "Ethereum and Smart Contracts" and "Crypto Economics and
  DAOs" are also "X and Y" topics.

## The real issue: the title and the content don't match

The page covers evolutionary computation as a whole; classic GA is only about a third of it.

| Group | Examples on the page | Approx. share |
|---|---|---|
| Classic GA and shared foundations | Mitchell, Goldberg, Holland, Whitley, PyGAD, Nature of Code | ~⅓ |
| GP / symbolic regression | Poli, Koza, PySR, TPOT, SRBench | ~⅙ |
| Neuroevolution and open-ended search | NEAT, novelty search, MAP-Elites, Karl Sims, MarI/O, Stanley & Lehman | ~⅕ |
| Evolution strategies | CMA-ES, Ha, Weng | small |
| LLM-guided program evolution | FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, ShinkaEvolve, Lehman's LLM mutation | small, growing |

If the page is ever split, GA vs GP is the wrong place to cut. More natural splits:

1. **Evolutionary Computation**, as the main topic: GA, ES/CMA-ES, multi-objective, theory.
2. **Neuroevolution and Open-Ended Search**: NEAT, novelty search, quality diversity. A separate
   research community (Stanley, Lehman, Clune) with its own books and talks.
3. **Program Evolution and Algorithm Discovery**: GP, symbolic regression, and the
   FunSearch/AlphaEvolve line. The fastest-growing group, and a natural link to the LLM page.

## Recommendation

- **Now:** keep one page. Either retitle it to something like "Evolutionary Computation
  (Genetic Algorithms and Genetic Programming)", or mark the GP-specific rows so readers who come
  for GP can find them.
- **Later:** if the neuroevolution or LLM-guided evolution groups grow past roughly 10
  resources, split those off first. GP would go with the LLM-guided group, not become its own
  page.
